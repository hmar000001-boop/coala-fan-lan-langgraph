## Why this is a single-chapter batch

Chapter 11 is a wide comparative foundation chapter. It must establish the historical catalog, the modern runtime layer, the specialist evidence layer, explicit bridge cases, and the independent E/D coding method before the focused mechanism studies in Chapters 12–17. Pairing it with another chapter would either compress the catalog or mix this broad landscape with one narrow residual mechanism. It is therefore planned as a standalone batch.

## Chapter architecture

1. The comparison problem and two architectural worlds.
2. The three corpus strata and bridge records.
3. The ten historical families, grouped into four pedagogical clusters.
4. The eight general runtime families and their actual engineering boundaries.
5. The forty-two mechanism-focused modern records grouped by mechanism domain and evidentiary status.
6. Four explicit historical–modern bridge cases and supporting records.
7. E1–E4 evidence relations and the precedence rule.
8. D0–D4 migration depth and the mechanism-specific boundary.
9. Independent E/D application through evidence packets and worked comparisons.
10. The high-level migration landscape, validity limits, common misreadings, and handoff to Chapters 12–18.

## Scope and source discipline

The outline is grounded in Fan & Lan §§I–V and VII-C; Figures 1–7; Tables I–IV; and Appendices A, E, and F. It uses the seven-field representation only as the already-introduced analytical vocabulary from Chapter 10. It does not reinterpret CoALA, make a live census claim, rank architectures, or teach B1–B6 in detail.

The outline keeps H (10 historical architectures), M-A (8 general runtimes), and M-B (42 mechanism-focused records) distinct from explicit bridge cases (Corpus B). It explicitly describes cross-indexed roles, particularly AIOS and the hybrid BDI–LLM record, rather than summing the roles as mutually exclusive populations.
