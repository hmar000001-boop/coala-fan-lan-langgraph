\chapter*{How to Read This Book}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{How to Read This Book}

The book is organized as a progression from conceptual foundations to mechanism analysis and then to one coherent engineering application:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Part I (Chapters 1--8)} develops CoALA from its conceptual foundations through its architectural dimensions, memory, internal and external actions, planning, evaluation, selection, and iterative decision process. Read this part without importing mechanisms from later research into the original framework.
  \item \textbf{Part II (Chapters 9--18)} studies Fan and Lan's mechanism-level review. It explains the analytical representation, historical and modern mechanism mappings, the residual mechanism bundles, the closed B5/GraSP case, and their synthesis. Keep the review's stated evidence and validity boundaries in view.
  \item \textbf{Part III (Chapters 19--20)} applies the ideas to one request-driven enterprise supply-chain disruption management system implemented in Java. Chapter 19 establishes engineering foundations; Chapter 20 develops the application. The implementation is framework-independent and is not a second theoretical framework or a collection of unrelated case studies.
\end{itemize}

The intended reader has basic programming knowledge. Each technical idea is introduced through a problem, followed by intuition, terminology, and a precise account. In Part III, distinguish the source-described mechanisms from the book's own engineering decisions, and treat the implementation as one worked system rather than a universal production template.
