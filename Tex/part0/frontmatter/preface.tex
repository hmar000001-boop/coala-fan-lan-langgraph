\chapter*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Preface}

Modern language agents are not simply calls to a language model. They are interactive systems that organize information, choose internal and external operations, respond to environmental feedback, and determine what should happen next. Understanding such systems requires both a vocabulary for their cognitive organization and a careful account of the mechanisms that implement their behavior.

This book develops that account in three parts. Part I explains the CoALA framework on its own terms, beginning with its architectural dimensions and progressing through memory, actions, planning, evaluation, selection, and iterative decision-making. Part II examines Fan and Lan's mechanism-level analysis as a separate contribution. It reconstructs the mechanisms, evidence boundaries, and migration gaps discussed in that review without retroactively attributing its later analyses to CoALA.

Part III moves from analysis to engineering through one request-driven supply-chain disruption-management application implemented in Java and operated against a deterministic simulated world. Chapter 19 establishes the domain and engineering foundations. Chapter 20 maps CoALA's architecture to the application's memory, action, and planning components. Chapters 21–23 examine the implemented B1/B3, B2, and B4/B6 control responsibilities. Chapter 24 follows their composition through LangGraph4j orchestration, LangChain4j model adapters, PostgreSQL persistence, and the test-backed simulated workflow. These technologies and design choices are engineering decisions for the teaching application; they are not mechanisms prescribed by either research paper. The system is a reference demonstration, not a deployed supply-chain integration.

Throughout the book, claims grounded in the two papers are distinguished from book-created examples and engineering proposals. The examples make concepts concrete; they are not evidence that the teaching application has been deployed or that the mechanisms have been independently validated in a separate experiment. Where a mechanism is incompletely documented, that uncertainty is preserved rather than filled with an assumed implementation.

The intended reader has basic programming knowledge and an interest in the design of complex software systems. The book aims to connect conceptual understanding, mechanism-level analysis, and disciplined engineering while keeping their different kinds of claims visible.
