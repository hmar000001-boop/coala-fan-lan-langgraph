\chapter*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Preface}

Modern language agents are not simply calls to a language model. They are interactive systems that organize information, choose internal and external operations, respond to environmental feedback, and determine what should happen next. Understanding such systems requires both a vocabulary for their cognitive organization and a careful account of the mechanisms that implement their behavior.

This book develops that account in three parts. Part I explains the CoALA framework on its own terms, beginning with its architectural dimensions and progressing through memory, actions, planning, evaluation, selection, and iterative decision-making. Part II examines Fan and Lan's mechanism-level analysis as a separate contribution. It reconstructs the mechanisms, evidence boundaries, and migration gaps discussed in that review without retroactively attributing its later analyses to CoALA.

Part III moves from analysis to engineering through one request-driven enterprise supply-chain disruption management system implemented in Java. Chapter 19 establishes the engineering foundations; Chapter 20 develops the application. The implementation is framework-independent. Its architecture, data contracts, control flow, persistence, safety boundaries, recovery procedures, and evaluation strategy are engineering decisions made for the example system—not mechanisms prescribed by either research paper.

Throughout the book, claims grounded in the two papers are distinguished from book-created examples and engineering proposals. The examples make concepts concrete; they are not evidence that a proposed production system has been deployed or empirically validated. Where a mechanism is incompletely documented, that uncertainty is preserved rather than filled with an assumed implementation.

The intended reader has basic programming knowledge and an interest in the design of complex software systems. The book aims to connect conceptual understanding, mechanism-level analysis, and disciplined engineering while keeping their different kinds of claims visible.
